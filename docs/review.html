A. Wrong number, no warning fix first

Each of these produces a finished-looking card. Nothing on it says the request was misread.

A1

Two ZIP codes joined by "and" count only 17-year-olds

$250 per child in 60637 and 60649ages 17 to 17 · 1,335 childrenShould be ages 0 to 17, 22,408 children. The comma form "60637, 60649" is correct.
$250 per child in 60637-1234ages 12 to 17A ZIP+4. Should be ages 0 to 17 for 60637.

WhyThe age-range pattern has no word boundaries, so it matches "37 and 60" inside the two ZIP codes and clamps both to 17.

FixStrip 5-digit tokens before parsing ages, and put word boundaries on both numbers.

A2

"for" after the place drops the geography, or lands in Kentucky

$250 per child in Chicago for ages 3 to 10ZIP code 40828 · Ages, KentuckyShould be Chicago, 57 ZIPs, ages 3 to 10.
$250 per child in Chicago for kids 3-10No ZIP codesSame request, different words.

WhyThe geography is taken from everything after the last locative preposition, and "for" is on that list. The clause becomes "ages 3 to 10", and Ages is a real town that opens it.

FixDrop "for" from the list, or try clauses from last to first and keep the first that resolves. Block town names that collide with grammar words unless a state follows.

A3

An unrecognized city falls through to the whole state

$250 per child in Saint Louis, MissouriMissouri · 1,035 ZIPs · 1,386,612 childrenThe city is 19 ZIPs. Off by a factor of 26.
$250 per child in Lexington, KentuckyKentucky · 779 ZIPs (before today's data fix)Now resolves to Lexington, 16 ZIPs, after the everyday-name fix below.

WhyWhen the city is not an index key, the state name in the same phrase matches on its own and wins.

FixIf the clause has leftover words before the state match, refuse and suggest instead of widening. "Saint" versus "St." is still a parser gap after the data fix.

A4

Two places named, only the last one counted

$250 per child in Chicago and Evanston, IllinoisEvanston only · 3 ZIPsChicago dropped without a word.
$250 per child in Cook County and Lake County, IllinoisLake County only · 28 ZIPsCook County's 167 ZIPs dropped.

FixDetect "and" or a comma between two resolved places; union them or refuse with a message.

A5

Total budgets and "million" read as a per-child gift

$1 million for kids in Chicago$1 per child · $537,496 total"million" is not parsed.
$250,000 total for kids in 60637$250,000 per child · $3,014,750,000 total"total" is not recognized.

FixParse million and billion. When "total" or "budget" appears, refuse, or divide by the child count and label the card as a per-child figure derived from a budget.

B. Refuses or quietly ignores when it should work friction

Nothing here prints a wrong number. Most produce a picker or an empty card. A few default silently, which the card does show but does not flag.

B1

A gift that is the prefix of the income cutoff disappears

$50 per child in Illinois under $50,000 median family incomeAdd a gift amountSame for $45 with $45,000 and $100 with $100,000. The gift loop skips any amount whose digits are a substring of the income clause. Compare positions, not substrings.
B2

State abbreviations are not understood

$250 per child in Peoria, ILPicker: Illinois, Arizona, ColoradoThe handoff promised "Peoria, IL". "Springfield, IL" and "Cook County, IL" behave the same.
$250 per child in Washington, DCPicker: the state, or Washington, Pennsylvania"District of Columbia" works. "NYC" and "LA" return nothing.

FixThe code comment says abbreviations are derived on first use, but nothing derives them. Build the map from the state list at load time; add DC, NYC and LA as aliases.

B3

Seventeen bare state names produce a state-or-city picker

Indiana, California, Florida, Ohio, Washington, Kansas, Nevada, Oregon, Wyoming, Delaware, Virginia, Iowa, Louisiana, Tennessee, Vermont, Maine and New Hampshire each share their name with a small town, so "$250 per child in California" asks whether you meant California, Pennsylvania. Honest, but heavy.

FixLet the state win when the same-named city is small, under ten ZIPs say. Keep the picker for New York and Washington, where the city is large.

B4

The "PG County" alias is dead

It targets "Prince Georges County, Maryland" but the index key keeps the apostrophe, so the alias is silently skipped at load.

B5

Age words that silently default to 0 to 17

"5 and up", "5+", "newborns", "teens" and "3 thru 10" all produce a card for ages 0 to 17. The card shows the range, so it is visible, but nothing says the phrase was not understood. Recognize "and up", "+" and "thru"; flag other age words.

B6

Income words that are silently ignored

"below $45,000 MFI" and "low income ZIP codes in Chicago" both return the whole city with no filter. Accept MFI; refuse "low income" with a hint to name a figure.

B7

Neighborhood plus city name reaches the wrong state

$250 per child in Hyde Park ChicagoPicker: Hyde Park, Pennsylvania or Utah"Lincoln Park, Chicago" offers New Jersey and Michigan. The neighborhood guard only knows compass words and downtown. When a resolved city also sits in the clause next to another matched place, refuse it the way "south side" is refused.
B8

New York boroughs

"Brooklyn" offers Brooklyn, Ohio and Indiana. "Manhattan" offers Kansas. "the Bronx" returns nothing. Every New York City ZIP carries the city name "New York". A data-side option is below.

C. Code review not reproduced, but real

C1

A failed shard fetch is swallowed

The shard loader catches any fetch error and returns an empty list. A statewide request that loses one of its sixty shard fetches prints a smaller number with no note. This is the one code path that can print a wrong figure with nothing visible. Surface the failure on the card, or fail the request.

C2

The sample flag is already off

It is hard-coded to false, so the "Sample data" badge never renders. The handoff said it stays on until reconciliation is recorded. This review is that record, so leaving it off is now defensible. Make it a decision rather than a default.

C3

"Median family income" on multi-ZIP cards is the median of ZIP medians

Unweighted. Not wrong, but the footnote should say "median across the ZIP codes shown" so it is not read as the area's household median.

C4

The income filter silently drops ZIPs with no income estimate

3,689 ZCTAs nationally have none. Add a note, as the age-gap note already does. Related: "ages 12 to 25" becomes "12 to 17" on the card with no note.

C5

CDN caching can serve stale data for twelve hours

The data is read from jsDelivr at the main branch, cached for twelve hours. After a data push, cards can read old numbers until the cache expires or is purged. I purged today's files. Recommend pinning to a commit or release tag and bumping on purpose, which also makes any printed card reproducible.

C6

Map basemap and export

The US basemap comes from us-atlas on jsDelivr at render time and degrades to "Map unavailable" if it fails. Fine. The repo's own geo layer can replace it when the two-panel map lands. Export uses html2canvas at 2x with embedded fonts and data-URI logos; verified working. Actual file save and clipboard depend on the viewer's browser, and the page's toasts already explain the fallbacks.

D. The data layer, and what was fixed today clean

  • 40 random ZCTAs: the single-year ages sum back to the published brackets exactly, and incomes match.
  • No ZCTA has a suppressed age bracket in this vintage. 3,689 have no income estimate.
  • Every row has a state. 28,791 of 33,772 have a city.
Fixed today. Consolidated city-counties carried Census legal names nobody types: Nashville-Davidson, Louisville/Jefferson County (also split across two names), Urban Honolulu, Boise City, Lexington-Fayette, Augusta-Richmond County, Athens-Clarke County, Macon-Bibb County and a few smaller ones. They are now indexed under their everyday names in both the data and the boundary files. Rebuilt, pushed, CDN purged. Nashville, Tennessee resolves to 28 ZIPs and Louisville, Kentucky to 35. A card can now read "Nashville, Tennessee" instead of "Nashville-Davidson, Tennessee".

Data-side options still open

  • New York City boroughs could go into the area column from the county field: Kings is Brooklyn, New York County is Manhattan, and so on. "Brooklyn" would then resolve through the existing area index with no parser change. It is a decision, because the area column was reserved for the neighborhood question.
  • Chicago community areas in the same column, if pilot cities are chosen.
