Putting the ZIP boundary on a real map, honestly

Donors who know their city want to see the actual ZIP code outline over streets, parks and neighborhood names. We already own the outline. This is the research on where the street layer comes from, what each source lets us do with an image that gets texted and kept, and what two working prototypes look like.

What the prototypes show

All four are ZIP 60637, with the ZCTA polygon from the repo drawn in brand navy. A, B and C load OpenStreetMap tiles live; D uses nothing but Census files.

Why licensing decides this, not cost

A card is not a web page. It is a PNG that lands in a text thread, gets forwarded, and may be printed. Any map inside it is stored indefinitely by people we do not control. That rules out two of the best-known services on their own terms, not ours.

  • Mapbox product terms allow map content to be cached on the requesting device for at most 30 days, populated by that device's own API calls. A forwarded card is neither.
  • Google Maps Platform terms prohibit storing or caching static map images outside narrow exceptions.
  • OpenStreetMap data is licensed for exactly this. An image made from it is a "produced work" you may distribute freely, as long as the card credits "© OpenStreetMap contributors".
  • Census TIGER data is public domain. No conditions at all.
The boundary stays honest. Both example sites draw USPS-style ZIP shapes. Ours is the Census ZCTA, which is what the child counts describe. Drawing the ZCTA on a real map makes the footnote's ZCTA sentence easier to understand, not harder.

A. OpenFreeMap with MapLibre recommended

OpenStreetMap vector tiles from a public server with no registration, no key, no usage limits and commercial use permitted. Required credit: "© OpenMapTiles © OpenStreetMap contributors". Rendered in the browser by MapLibre GL, one more script next to d3 and html2canvas. Before export the map canvas is copied into an image so html2canvas captures it; the prototype does this and the export works.

Risks and covers. The server is run by one person and funded by sponsorship, with no SLA. The generator waits for the map's idle event with a timeout of a few seconds and falls back to the outline panel. For a guaranteed service later, self-host from OpenFreeMap's weekly planet images, or cut a US extract with Protomaps and serve one static file. Neither changes the card.

B. Protomaps step two

Same data and rendering model, run by a small company. Hosted API needs a key and is free for non-commercial use, commercial through GitHub sponsorship from about $14 a month. Its value is the self-host path: one PMTiles file per region on any static host or CDN. The natural upgrade if A's public server ever becomes a concern.

C. Geoapify Static Maps simplest integration

One URL returns a finished PNG with our polygon drawn on it, up to 4,096 pixels, GeoJSON overlay supported. Their published terms explicitly allow caching, storing and redistributing images with OpenStreetMap attribution. Free tier is 3,000 credits a day. Costs: a client-exposed key, restrictable by domain, and a small vendor in the loop for every card. The right pick if the design side would rather not run a map library. Not prototyped because it needs an account.

D and E. Mapbox and Google not for a kept image

Both produce beautiful maps. Mapbox's product terms cap on-device caching at 30 days and require each device to populate its own cache. Google's forbid storing static map images. Either could be licensed for print or offline use through sales, but that is a contract, not a checkbox.

F and G. MapTiler, Stadia, Esri

MapTiler has no static maps on its free plan. Stadia's free tier is non-commercial and static maps start at $20 a month. Esri gives 2 million basemap tiles a month free with a "Powered by Esri" credit; I did not confirm its terms on stored images. Viable paid alternatives to A if a contract is preferred over a volunteer service. None is better than A for this need.

H. Census TIGER only fallback aesthetic

Every road with names and classes, every water body, area and point landmarks (parks, campuses, cemeteries, hospitals, stations, museums). Clipped to the 60637 area and drawn by the card's own SVG pipeline it is recognizable: Washington Park, the University of Chicago, the Midway Plaisance, Jackson Park, the Museum of Science and Industry, the Dan Ryan, the lakefront.

What it lacks: neighborhood names (TIGER has none), building footprints, and the polish of a designed basemap. What it costs: local roads are about 14 MB zipped for Cook County alone and several gigabytes nationally, too much to commit. Workable as pre-clipped bundles for pilot cities, or nationally with primary and secondary roads only, which loses the neighborhood streets. The Census's live query service returned errors in my tests. Keep H as the fallback look and as a source of landmark labels, not as the primary.

What this means for the design brief

  • Single ZIP cards. The detail panel becomes a real map: basemap, neighbor ZIPs tinted, the ZIP filled and outlined, framed to the ZIP with about 15% padding. Caption unchanged.
  • Several ZIPs in one area. Same treatment, framed to the selection, while the selection is under roughly 25 km across. Beyond that the basemap stops adding information and the existing outline-only panel is the better picture. That threshold becomes a new rung in the fallback ladder.
  • City, county and state requests. Keep the outline-only panel. A basemap under a whole city or state is noise at card size.
  • Footnote. When a basemap is drawn, append "Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, © OpenMapTiles." Keep the ZCTA sentence.
  • Failure. If tiles do not load within a few seconds, or WebGL is missing, render the outline-only panel and drop the OSM credit.
  • Panel size. The even-weight recommendation stands and gets stronger. A real map needs the room. Side by side, stacked or diagonal remains the designer's call.

Data side, available now

  • Nothing is required for A beyond what is already in geo/. The prototype reads the repo's own polygons.
  • Optional: TIGER landmarks (parks, campuses, hospitals, stations) sharded by ZIP prefix, about 100 MB nationally, so the card can label landmarks in its own type even when the basemap is unavailable.
  • Optional, pilot cities: neighborhood polygons and names into the area column and a geo/areas/ folder. The same open decision as before, now with a concrete use.
