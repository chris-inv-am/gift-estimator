The map row: two panels, even weight

Today the card carries one large US map with the state highlighted, and a small state thumbnail beneath it. Before real boundaries existed, the thumbnail could only repeat what the big map already said, so it earned little space.

With boundaries, the two panels do different jobs. The national panel orients: where in the country, and it is the brand's frame for a national donor base. The detail panel carries the claim: which part of which city the number describes. That is the panel a Chicago donor actually looks at.

Recommendation

  • Equal width, side by side, same height, aligned top edges. When the request is a single city or smaller, the detail panel may grow to 60% of the row. It should never be smaller than the national panel.
  • The detail panel always gets a one-line caption naming exactly what is drawn. The national panel keeps the state highlight and adds a dot at the selection.
  • Draw order in the detail panel: frame fill, every ZIP that belongs to the frame as a light tint with hairline borders, selected ZIPs in brand navy, frame outline on top.
  • Fit the frame's bounding box into the panel with 6 to 8% padding. A plain equirectangular projection with the x axis scaled by cos(center latitude) is indistinguishable from anything fancier at city scale.
Shade only what is counted. A selected ZIP with a suppressed count in the requested age range is drawn outline-only, never filled. The footnote already says those children are not counted. The map has to agree with it.

Fallback ladder

Work down the list and take the first rule that applies. The frame is the outline drawn behind the ZIPs. Every fallback changes the caption, so the reader is never shown a partial or empty panel without being told.

  1. Selection spans more than one state, or the ZIPs are far apartBounding box wider than about 3 degrees. Drop the detail panel; the national panel carries every dot at full width. This is the current single-map layout, so it is already built.
  2. Whole-state requestFrame is the state outline with no ZIP shading. Fourteen hundred shaded ZIPs is just a filled state.
  3. Whole-county requestFrame is the county outline, the county's ZIPs tinted. City outlines inside it optional, as hairlines.
  4. Whole-city requestFrame is the city outline, all of its ZIPs tinted, none emphasized.
  5. One or more ZIPs, all assigned to one cityFrame is the city outline, those ZIPs filled navy, the rest of the city tinted. The 60637 case.
  6. ZIPs with no city, or in different cities within one countyFrame is the county outline. Rural ZIPs have a blank city column and land here.
  7. ZIPs in different counties within one stateFrame is the state outline, selected ZIPs filled. If they are too small to see, rule 9 applies.
  8. Frame outline missingEight old Connecticut counties (the state moved to planning regions) and about 25 cities that merged or dissolved since 2020. Step up one level: city to county, county to state. If that is missing too, rule 1.
  9. Selection too small to seeSelected bounding-box area over frame area. Above 0.5%, fine. Between 0.05% and 0.5%, fill the shape and add a ring marker at its centroid. Below 0.05%, zoom the frame to the selection padded four times over, draw the frame line clipped, and caption it as a partial view.
  10. Anything elseThe state thumbnail exactly as the card draws it today, captioned "shown at state level". A vague map beats a wrong one, and a wrong one is worse than none.

What the geo layer is

GeoJSON, longitude and latitude, coordinates rounded to four decimals (about 11 metres). Census 1:500,000 cartographic boundaries, simplified a further 25% for thumbnail rendering. Not for legal boundary use.

geo/us.json                    all states in one file (~250 KB) for the national panel
geo/states/IL.json             one state outline, with bbox
geo/counties/IL/index.json     "Cook County, Illinois" -> { geoid, file, bbox }
geo/counties/IL/17031.json     one county outline
geo/places/IL/index.json       "Chicago, Illinois" -> { geoid, file, bbox }
geo/places/IL/1714000.json     one city outline (~3 KB)
geo/zcta/606.json              every ZCTA shape with prefix 606, props { zip }
geo/index.json                 source, vintage, feature counts, state list
  • Keys match the data index exactly. The generator already resolves "Chicago, Illinois" to ZIP prefixes. The same string, looked up in that state's geo index, gives the outline file. No new matching logic; the state is in the key, so you know which index to read.
  • ZIP shapes shard like the numbers. A request for 60637 reads data/606.json and geo/zcta/606.json. One prefix file is 20 to 200 KB.
  • The city line is context; the crosswalk is the truth. Which ZIPs count toward "Chicago" is decided by the city column in the data rows. A ZIP assigned to Chicago can poke outside the city line, and that is honest: the count includes the whole ZIP.
  • ZIP shapes are the 2020 vintage because that is the only vintage the Census publishes them for, and it is the ZCTA definition the 2019 to 2023 ACS uses. Shapes and numbers describe the same ZIP.
  • Every index entry and state file carries a bbox as west, south, east, north, so a panel can be framed before its shape loads.

Fetch sequence for one card

  1. The parser resolves the request to a set of ZIPs plus the geography named: ZIP, city, county or state.
  2. Load data/<prefix>.json for each prefix and compute the numbers. Nothing here changes.
  3. Pick the frame from the ladder. Load its outline and geo/zcta/<prefix>.json for the selected prefixes.
  4. Render. If any geo fetch fails or returns nothing, fall back per the ladder. Never render an empty panel.

Reconciliation so far

The v5 card for 60637, ages 0 to 17 at $250 per child, shows 12,059 children and a $3,014,750 total gift. Both match the four raw Census brackets exactly (2,942 + 3,645 + 2,972 + 2,500) and the row in data/606.json. Median family income on the card matches the row too.

Known gaps

  • City assignment by land share can flip a ZIP whose postal name is a small city inside a larger one. 90210 resolves to Los Angeles, not Beverly Hills.
  • The area column is blank nationwide, pending the neighborhood decision. Chicago publishes its 77 community areas on the city data portal; that is the source if "south side" becomes a drawn region as well as a count.
  • Connecticut counties and about 25 dissolved cities have numbers but no outline. Ladder rule 8 handles them.
  • Alaska, Hawaii and Puerto Rico insets are a rendering choice. geo/us.json has every state and territory as its own feature.

Still open, not ours to guess

  • The footnote validation sentence. Currently "Validate before formal decisions." Legal or comms decide.
  • Whether the area column is populated nationally or in pilot cities only.
  • The "sample data" flag stays on the cards until the dashboard reconciliation is recorded. The manifest says the data is real; the card flag is a separate switch on the generator side.
